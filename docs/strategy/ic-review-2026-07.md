## What company this actually is

**One sentence:** Prism ingests engineering exhaust — GitHub, AI coding sessions, deploys,
AI invoices — to produce an audit-grade answer to "is our AI spend making the software better?"

**The real business:** the **justification business** — defensible numbers for executives who
must explain an exploding AI budget line to a board. Nearest ancestral category: engineering
intelligence (Jellyfish, LinearB, DX) — historically a graveyard (mid ACVs, champion-dependent,
developer-resented, no breakouts). The bet: "AI ROI" turns a mediocre category urgent because
the measured thing now has its own P&L line. Thesis, not yet evidence.

## Five-year success case

The **AI P&L system of record**: neutral layer every board deck cites. $40–80M ARR; 400–1,200
mid-market/enterprise orgs; CTO buyer + CFO approver; moat = cross-org benchmark data +
system-of-record lock-in; category ("AI ROI") must be created. A company whose moat is a
dataset it doesn't have, in a category that doesn't exist, sold with a motion the founder has
never run — i.e., a seed.

## The single existential flaw

**The roadmap spends twelve months buying certainty about measurement and zero months buying
certainty about demand.** First external user ~month 5; first prospective customer ~month 8;
every ship gate validates code, not willingness to pay. Three model-lab iterations happened
before one external human saw a number. The spec quality is the tell: this founder retreats
into rigor when the risk is elsewhere. (→ fixed by the six-month experiment plan.)

cost. Open question: is spanning a company, or a two-quarter integration once we prove the category?

## Unfair advantage

**None exists today.** The honesty posture (confidence gates, honest nulls, no invented
dollars) is a wedge incumbents are business-model-unable to copy — but a posture is not a
moat. The real moats (graph tenure, intervention corpus, referee status) must be
manufactured, and the window is ~18–24 months.

## The reductions

- **One page:** the monthly AI ROI statement — billed cost with coverage badges on the left; AI-assisted share + reverts-vs-human-baseline with evidence badges on the right. A CTO forwards it to a CFO unedited. Everything else is machinery.
- **Six months:** see [next-6-months.md](next-6-months.md) (adopted).
- **Four engineers:** E1 ingest/link · E2 scoring/statement · E3 run-cost lane · E4 platform + partner rotation. Founder sells and stops writing scoring code by month two.

## The memo numbers

- **Year-3 ARR:** base $1–2M · bull $8–12M · bear $0.
- **P($100M ARR company):** ~4%. **P(complete failure):** ~60–65%.
- **Founder risk:** solo, key-person, research-brained; perfection-before-contact pattern; needs a commercial partner or an unnatural transformation within 12 months.

## Verdict

**Pass at $3M today — with defined re-entry:** (1) five design partners generating the ROI
statement from their own data, (2) two paid pilots at any price, (3) a demonstrated
multi-tool ingest path, (4) evidence the founder can sell or a partner who can. Bring those
in six months and it's a competitive term sheet.

## What the arc later corrected in this review

- "People buy dashboards" → the statement is the installation vector; the engine (question → evidence → verified answer → learning) is the company.
- The adoption loop (recommend → verify → learn) was underweighted → it's the corpus factory.
